# muddytires — Competitor Landscape 2026

**muddytires.ca** is a free, no-login map of Canadian camping/vanlife POIs. Its differentiators: (1) **free + no login** to browse and contribute; (2) an **"Oracle"** that gives Crown-land camping *legality* sourced to official provincial/federal references — not "someone camped here once," but "is this legal, and here's the rule"; (3) iOverlander-style **clean clustering** over **~51k Canadian POIs** (free dispersed/Crown sites, water, dump, EV charging, gym, toilets, lockers, etc.); (4) **frictionless free community contribution**.

This card profiles the six closest competitors against that edge. Every factual claim keeps its source URL or is marked `(inference)`. It ends with a synthesis of the positioning themes that beat the *whole* field, plus an honest accounting of where rivals beat us — so we close the gap rather than spin it.

---

## 1. The Dyrt

US camping-discovery platform, freemium, self-described "#1 camping app" with ~4M reviews/photos/tips, ~45k reviewed campgrounds, ~30M visits/year.

- **Pricing.** Freemium. Free tier browses campgrounds, reads/writes reviews, basic search/map (no login to browse; account needed to contribute). Paid "The Dyrt PRO" = USD $59.99/year after a 7-day free trial, **auto-renew ON by default**, credit card required for the trial (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311-How-much-does-PRO-cost-What-is-the-renewal-cost ; https://thedyrt.com/pro). Discounted promo pricing ($35.99/yr, $17.99/yr 50%-off) circulates via affiliate/deal channels (https://slickdeals.net/f/17685669-thedyrt-com-50-off-annual-membership-17-99). The free-camping map layers are **PRO-only**.
- **Free / boondocking support.** PARTIAL **and paywalled**. Dispersed/boondocking discovery is PRO-only: "PRO Maps (BLM & USFS) to help you determine where you can boondock" and a "Free Camping Collection" of "Over 5,000 staff-verified free camping locations" (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311 ; https://thedyrt.com/pro). Wikipedia confirms the split — free = reviews/photos/search; PRO = dispersed-camping maps, offline maps, trip planning (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dyrt). Boondocking layers are **US public-land categories (BLM/USFS) only**.
- **Canadian coverage.** **NONE.** Official FAQ: "the Dyrt does not cover international campgrounds, but we hope to include both Mexico and Canada in future versions" (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/13960212558612-Does-The-Dyrt-cover-campgrounds-in-Canada-or-Mexico). Wikipedia corroborates operations are "limited to the United States only" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dyrt). Crown land is a Canadian land-tenure concept the BLM/USFS model cannot represent `(inference)`.
- **Key weakness.** For a free-Canadian-Crown-land user the gap is total: (1) zero Canadian data → an empty map (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/13960212558612-Does-The-Dyrt-cover-campgrounds-in-Canada-or-Mexico); (2) even free-site discovery is locked behind $59.99/yr and modeled only as US BLM/USFS — no Crown-land legality, no Ontario 21-day rule, no non-resident permits (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311 ; cf. https://www.ontario.ca/page/non-resident-crown-land-camping-and-green-zones); (3) auto-renew ON by default is a billing-friction pattern. No public review specifically complains "lacks Canadian Crown-land" — because Canadians don't use it for that; the weakness is structural/coverage, shown by the FAQ, not a complaint thread `(inference)`.
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires is free, no-login, 100% Canadian, and the only product citing official provincial/federal sources for Crown-land legality (the Oracle) — where The Dyrt shows Canadians an empty map and paywalls US-only boondocking layers behind $59.99/yr.
- **Where they beat us.** Honestly, several things. (1) **Review depth** — ~4M reviews/photos/tips, ~45k reviewed campgrounds (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dyrt); muddytires' ~51k POIs are mostly points, not review-heavy. (2) **Booking + discounts** — commission-free Instant Book, "up to 40% off and no booking fees," reusable "Dyrt Alerts" for sold-out sites (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311); muddytires does no reservations. (3) **Scale/polish** — ~30M visits/yr, mature iOS/Android apps with offline PRO maps and road-trip planning.

## 2. Campendium (merged into Roadtrippers / Roadpass)

- **Pricing.** Freemium, **login required**. Free ($0): 70,000+ campgrounds, reviews/photos, free & dispersed search, save favorites/fuel stops (https://campendium.com/membership/). Paid "RV PRO": $59.99/year or $19.99/month (https://campendium.com/membership/); App Store shows in-app "Roadtrippers Premium $59.99" / "Annual Supporter $23.99" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/campendium-rv-tent-camping/id1191380095). 7-day trial on annual only (https://campendium.com/membership/). Paid tier is now effectively Roadtrippers/Roadpass Pro at $59.99/yr (https://www.irv2.com/forums/f93/roadtrippers-closing-campendium-app-638147.html).
- **Free / boondocking support.** **Genuine strength.** Free tier includes "Free & dispersed camping search (BLM, USFS, National Parks)" plus boondocking (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/campendium-rv-tent-camping/id1191380095 ; https://campendium.com/membership/) — dispersed sites are NOT paywalled. But 16,000+ "overnight RV parking spots" are gated behind RV PRO, and community review data is now partly behind the Roadtrippers paywall — a user complaint that the community "let everyone submit campsites and reviews only to put it behind a paywall" (https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1191380095/campendium-rv-tent-camping/reviews).
- **Canadian coverage.** Claims "US and Canada" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/campendium-rv-tent-camping/id1191380095) but is **heavily US-centric**. Homepage state dropdown lists only US states/territories with an "I am not a U.S. resident" catch-all and **no Canadian provinces** (https://www.campendium.com/). Its own Canada guide concedes "most of Canada's public land is not vehicle accessible," offers only experiential advice, and gives **no province-by-province Crown rules and no official citations** (https://go.campendium.com/camp-for-free-in-canada/). Third-party guides recommend iOverlander over Campendium for Canadian boondocking (https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/how-to-find-free-camping-usa-canada/).
- **Key weakness.** No sourced/authoritative Crown-land legality + a US-centric dataset, compounded by paywalling community data and platform instability. Canada content is personal advice with zero official citations and no provincial breakdown (https://go.campendium.com/camp-for-free-in-canada/). Roadtrippers/Roadpass shut down the Campendium app and folded it into a $59.99/yr subscription, drawing backlash — one user paid ~$60 then found the app shut down, and the redesign initially removed the map (later restored) (https://www.irv2.com/forums/f93/roadtrippers-closing-campendium-app-638147.html ; https://www.rvnetwork.com/topic/149230-campendiumcom-looks-like-it-is-gone-another-great-camping-info-website-gone/). Requires login, lacks Crown-land legality, pushes a paid US-focused RV subscription `(inference)`.
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires is free, no-login, and gives sourced, province-by-province Crown-land legality (the Oracle) — exactly the authoritative Canadian layer Campendium's US-centric, paywalled, login-gated, advice-only product lacks.
- **Where they beat us.** Honestly, its RV dataset and review depth dwarf muddytires: 70,000+ campgrounds and 150,000+ first-hand RVer reviews with cell-coverage maps, rig-length/hookup details, nightly rates, dump/fuel/charging data, and RV-safe GPS routing by height/weight/propane (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/campendium-rv-tent-camping/id1191380095 ; https://campendium.com/membership/), plus a 4.8-star rating across ~21,000 ratings (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/campendium-rv-tent-camping/id1191380095). muddytires has no rich review corpus and no RV-routing.

## 3. AllStays Camp & RV

Long-running US-origin camping/RV locator (allstays.com): offline, downloadable database of 37,000+ campgrounds (US & Canada), public-lands/BLM/National Forest sites, Walmarts, rest areas, dump stations. Historically a one-time ~$10 iOS purchase; converted to freemium subscription after a 2022 ownership change; Android dropped after 2022, restored October 2025.

- **Pricing.** Freemium subscription. Free to download with a usable free tier (search 37,000+ campgrounds, details, reviews, filters, offline maps, NOAA weather). "Pro" tiers (Mar/May 2026): $9.99 / 1 mo, $19.99 / 3 mo, $34.99 / yr, each with a 3-day free trial. Pro unlocks Favorites/saving, photo & review search, custom filters, multi-device sync, full web access, priority updates, bridge-clearance checking. Legacy ~$10 one-time buyers could be grandfathered via apps@allstays.com (sources: https://www.thervgeeks.com/allstays-camp-and-rv/ ; https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/ — pricing "can change without notice," verify in store).
- **Free / boondocking support.** **Genuine** — a "Public Lands" filter surfaces BLM and National Forest dispersed sites "that don't appear on Google Maps," plus "primitive hike-in/boat-in/rough road spots that other apps exclude," dispersed sites, free overnights, dump stations, and free-overnight retail; the review cites "60,000+ location pins" (https://www.allstays.com/apps/camprv.htm ; https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/). Limitation: a "locator and aggregator — not a navigation tool for rough BLM roads," lacks MVUM data, and provides **no legality/regulation sourcing** for whether a dispersed site is legal.
- **Canadian coverage.** Partial and US-centric. Official page advertises "Over 37,000 campgrounds: (US & Canada)" and a reviewer says "it has quite a lot of information for Canada" (https://www.allstays.com/apps/camprv.htm ; https://www.thervgeeks.com/allstays-camp-and-rv/). But the free-camping model is built on US constructs (BLM, National Forest, Walmart/Cracker Barrel overnights) — **no sourced Crown-land legality, no province-by-province rules**. Crown land is absent from all official and review material reviewed `(inference)`.
- **Key weakness.** Three exploitable gaps: (1) it now **costs money** for the useful features — the one-time ~$10 → $9.99/mo–$34.99/yr conversion drew "subscription model shock" complaints, and the free tier blocks saving/Favorites/sync (https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/); (2) **zero Crown-land legality** — US-public-lands-oriented, no sourced "is camping legal here?" anywhere in Canada; (3) **data-accuracy decay** — "Fees, hours, and overnight-parking policies change faster than any volunteer-maintained database can track," and "GPS requires cell service" (https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/). It also lost Android entirely for ~3 years (2022–Oct 2025).
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires is free, no-login, and answers the one question AllStays can't — "is this Crown-land site actually legal?" — with an Oracle citing official provincial/federal sources, on a clean clustered Canadian map, while AllStays charges up to $34.99/yr with no Crown-land legality at all.
- **Where they beat us.** Honestly, breadth and offline reliability: a fully **downloadable on-device database that works without cell service** (critical 40 miles from signal), and far more POI categories with deep RV-specific data muddytires lacks — bridge/overpass clearance heights, dump stations at scale, propane, RV dealers/repair, truck stops, weight/size routing. Battle-tested over years with a 1,000,000+ user reporting base and mature filters (https://www.allstays.com/apps/camprv.htm ; https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/).

## 4. RV Parky

Community-sourced RV park & campground finder (iOS, Android, web) covering USA, Canada, Mexico.

- **Pricing.** Freemium. Free tier: basic account with community info & reviews, but **trip planning is capped** (official Pro page and Apple listing say "Plan up to 3 stops"; one blog reports 5 — sources disagree on the number, but planning IS gated). Paid "RV Parky Pro": $4.99/month or $19.99/year unlocks unlimited stops, RV-friendly routing, saved trips/favorites, no ads — confirmed across the official page, the App Store IAP listing, and a third-party review (https://www.rvparky.com/rvparkypro ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271 ; https://www.getawaycouple.com/rvparky/). Historically fully free before this model.
- **Free / boondocking support.** **Weak / paid-park-centric.** The official Pro page makes **zero mention** of free camping, boondocking, dispersed camping, or Crown land (https://www.rvparky.com/rvparkypro). App Store location types: "RV parks, campgrounds, rest areas, Walmarts, Camping Worlds, Cabella's, Cracker Barrels, and Truck Stops" — free dispersed/Crown camping NOT listed (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271). Some blogs note "National Forest camping" and "overnight parking" icons and call it useful "for finding good information on campgrounds and boondocking" (https://drivinvibin.com/2020/12/12/what-is-rv-parky/) — so not literally zero, but overwhelmingly paid parks/retail/rest-stop, no Crown-land legality layer `(inference)`.
- **Canadian coverage.** Nominal but thin and US-centric. Canada is named as covered — listings span "USA, Canada, and Mexico" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271). But **no Crown-land data, no provincial/federal legality sourcing**, no Canada-specific layer on the Pro page (https://www.rvparky.com/rvparkypro). Data is community-sourced "as users travel" (https://drivinvibin.com/2020/12/12/what-is-rv-parky/), so Canadian density depends on US-majority contributions and is sparse/unsourced `(inference)`.
- **Key weakness.** Core trip-planning is paywalled (unlimited stops/routing/saving need $4.99/mo or $19.99/yr; free is capped at ~3 stops) and there is no sourced Crown-land legality — for our target user it offers paid RV parks and retail overnight parking, not legality-verified free dispersed sites. Reviewers report it feels "orphaned by the developer" and flag accuracy/completeness issues; an App Store review says the developer "locked app features...app will not work for more than 3 stops...forcing people to upgrade" (https://www.rvparky.com/rvparkypro ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271 ; https://drivinvibin.com/2020/12/12/what-is-rv-parky/ ; https://rvforums.com/threads/rv-parky.14805/).
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires is 100% free, no-login, and the only product giving Canadian Crown-land camping a sourced legality answer (the Oracle cites official provincial/federal rules) — where RV Parky paywalls trip-planning and treats Canada as an afterthought of US-centric paid-park data.
- **Where they beat us.** A mature, established multi-platform product (native iOS + Android + web) with structured RV-specific data muddytires does not focus on — RV-friendly routing/navigation, trip-planning with saved itineraries, rich amenity/hookup detail (full hookups, big-rig access), and retail overnight-parking coverage (Walmart, Cracker Barrel, Cabela's, truck stops) across US/Canada/Mexico, plus a long-running review/photo community and a 4.0-star rating from 438 ratings (https://www.rvparky.com/rvparkypro ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271).

## 5. Outly (by Outside Analytics)

Free crowdsourced offline mapping app for camping, hunting, fishing, off-roading and water activities. iOS/Android + web.

- **Pricing.** **FREE** — 100% free, no in-app purchases, no subscription. Confirmed on the App Store listing ("Free", no IAP) and by Backpacker ("Free, Crowdsourced App"); reviewers position it as the free alternative to onX ("$30 a year") (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outly/id1460401025 ; https://www.backpacker.com/gear-item/outly/ ; https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460401025/outly/reviews). No paid tier exists.
- **Free / boondocking support.** **YES — genuine, free-camping-first.** App Store lists "dispersed camping locations and federal campsites" and renders public-lands layers (National Forest / BLM) where dispersed camping is legal; reviews confirm real free-camping use ("We disperse camped a lot this summer... found new camping spots") (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outly/id1460401025 ; https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460401025/outly/reviews). Not a booking funnel.
- **Canadian coverage.** **Essentially none / US-centric.** App Store focuses on the western US (federal campsites, National Forest, BLM), no mention of Canada or Crown land. Telling proxy: the in-app "wildlife" feature only offers "a few predominantly western US states," with a reviewer unable to use it because "My state isn't one of them." Its data model is built on US state/federal land units, not Canadian provincial Crown land — structurally incompatible with sourced Canadian Crown-land legality (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outly/id1460401025 ; https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460401025/outly/reviews) `(inference)`.
- **Key weakness.** Wrong country, wrong legality model. (1) No Canadian coverage / no Crown-land data and a US-state-locked data model. (2) **No sourced legality at all** — shows BLM/USFS boundaries but doesn't cite official sources for where camping is legal; muddytires' Oracle has no analog here. (3) Data-accuracy complaints — identical/wrong stock photos on unrelated sites ("Don't believe the pictures!"). (4) **No location search bar** — repeatedly requested. (5) Crowdsourced data-entry friction even within the US (forced to mark 2/4WD access on a foot-only site) (https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460401025/outly/reviews ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outly/id1460401025).
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires gives Canadians what Outly can't: real Canadian POIs with Crown-land legality cited to official provincial/federal sources (the Oracle) — free, no-login, clean clustering — where Outly is a US/BLM-tenure tool with no Canada data and no sourced legality.
- **Where they beat us.** Honestly, several. (1) **Offline maps** — full offline downloads with live GPS positioning out of cell service, a core boondocking capability our web map doesn't match. (2) **Native iOS/Android apps** with strong distribution (4.7/5 from 1,000+ ratings) vs a web map. (3) **Multi-activity breadth** — hunting unit boundaries, cell-coverage overlays, off-road difficulty (2WD/4WD/dirt-bike), swimming holes/water flow. (4) Mature US public-lands boundary data praised as "excellent... precise boundary lines" (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outly/id1460401025 ; https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1460401025/outly/reviews ; https://www.backpacker.com/gear-item/outly/).

## 6. FreeCampsites.net

Free, community-driven, map-based web search engine for free/cheap camping on public lands. No native app (the Android "app" is a webview wrapper); no iOS app. Strong US public-lands focus; lists some Canadian sites with no Crown-land legality layer.

- **Pricing.** **100% FREE.** No paid/freemium tiers; no account required to search — "a simple, map based search engine to find free and cheap camping areas," with cost categories describing the *campsites* (Free / Pay ≤$12 / Pay >$12 / Permit Required), not the service (https://freecampsites.net/). Corroborated as "completely free to use, no account is required to search" (https://www.thewaywardhome.com/best-boondocking-app/ ; https://www.mortonsonthemove.com/best-boondocking-apps/).
- **Free / boondocking support.** **YES — its core purpose and strongest field.** Built around free dispersed/public-land camping: "Our focus is on public lands... Forest Service land, BLM... WMA's... and county or city parks," plus Walmart lots and rest areas (https://freecampsites.net/ ; https://www.thewaywardhome.com/best-boondocking-app/). Surfaces free dispersed sites with cell-coverage reports and photos in reviews. This is where it competes most directly with muddytires.
- **Canadian coverage.** **Weak / US-centric.** Built on US federal-land designations (BLM, Forest Service, WMA) with no Canadian equivalents on the homepage or Canada page (https://freecampsites.net/ ; https://freecampsites.net/#!Canada). It does have a Canada page listing some user-submitted Canadian sites (https://offtracktravel.ca/free-camping-in-canada/), but **no Crown-land legality layer, no official sourcing, no per-province legal status**. Canadian guides recommend it only as one tool and direct users to official provincial Crown-land atlases (e.g. Ontario CLUPA) for actual legality (https://offtracktravel.ca/free-camping-in-canada/). Canadian density is markedly thinner than US data `(inference)`.
- **Key weakness.** No sourced Crown-land legality for Canada + stale/conflicting data + clunky mobile UX. (1) **Legality** — doesn't verify or cite legal status; Canadian guides explicitly route users to "official provincial or territorial maps" as "the most legal and appropriate way to find crown land," not FreeCampsites (https://offtracktravel.ca/free-camping-in-canada/). (2) **Data accuracy** — "often quite a lot of conflicting or out-of-date information in the reviews" (https://www.thewaywardhome.com/best-boondocking-app/); a "mixture of excellent and less than good sites... some are overused, busy and noisy" (https://offtracktravel.ca/free-camping-in-canada/). (3) **Mobile/UX** — "the map function can be a little clumsy" on a phone, interface "outdated," Android app merely opens the website (https://www.thewaywardhome.com/best-boondocking-app/ ; https://www.mortonsonthemove.com/best-boondocking-apps/). The exploitable gap: it tells you a site MIGHT be free, never whether camping there is LEGAL, and its Canadian data is thin and aging.
- **muddytires counter.** muddytires gives a Canadian what FreeCampsites can't: an Oracle citing official provincial/federal sources to say whether Crown-land camping is actually LEGAL there — not just whether someone once camped free — on a clean, mobile-first clustering map of ~51k Canadian POIs, free and no-login.
- **Where they beat us.** A far larger, more battle-tested **US** community review corpus — years of reviews with cell-coverage reports, photos, road-condition notes, and busy/noisy social proof per site (https://www.thewaywardhome.com/best-boondocking-app/). For the US public-lands market its data depth and review volume exceed what a Canada-focused newcomer can match, with a proven multi-year contributor crowd. (Honest: our edge is Canada + legality; theirs is US breadth + review depth.)

## Synthesis

### Positioning themes that beat the WHOLE field

**1. "The only one that tells you if it's *legal* — sourced." — The Oracle is a category of one.**
Every rival answers "did someone camp free here?" None answers "is camping here legal under the actual rule, and here's the official source." The Dyrt, Campendium, AllStays, RV Parky, Outly, and FreeCampsites are all built on US land-tenure constructs (BLM/USFS/National Forest) that **cannot represent Canadian Crown land at all** — and even the free-camping-first tools (Outly, FreeCampsites) explicitly cite *zero* legality and get routed back to official provincial atlases by Canadian guides themselves (https://offtracktravel.ca/free-camping-in-canada/). This is not a feature gap; it's a structural one. muddytires owns sourced Canadian Crown-land legality with no competitor in the space.

**2. "Free, no login, no paywall on the thing that matters." — Friction is the field's open wound.**
The whole paid field paywalls exactly the feature that overlaps our core: The Dyrt locks free-site discovery behind $59.99/yr with auto-renew ON (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311); Campendium requires login and folded into a $59.99/yr Roadtrippers subscription that drew shut-down backlash (https://www.irv2.com/forums/f93/roadtrippers-closing-campendium-app-638147.html); AllStays converted a one-time ~$10 app into a $34.99/yr subscription ("subscription model shock," https://boondockorbust.com/resources/allstays-camp-and-rv-app-for-camping/); RV Parky caps trip-planning at ~3 stops to force a $19.99/yr upgrade (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rv-parky-parks-campgrounds/id695969271). The only genuinely-free rivals (Outly, FreeCampsites) **have no Canada**. So "free + no login + Canadian" is a position literally nobody else occupies.

**3. "Built for Canada, not bolted onto it." — Every rival treats Canada as an afterthought or omits it.**
The Dyrt: none (https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/13960212558612-Does-The-Dyrt-cover-campgrounds-in-Canada-or-Mexico). Campendium: claims Canada but the state dropdown has no provinces and the Canada guide is uncited advice (https://www.campendium.com/ ; https://go.campendium.com/camp-for-free-in-canada/). AllStays/RV Parky: "Canada included" but US-construct data, thin and unsourced. Outly: US-state-locked. FreeCampsites: thin user-submitted points, US land categories. A clustered, Canada-first map of ~51k Canadian POIs with the right land-tenure model is a defensible home-field advantage — third-party guides already recommend iOverlander over the US incumbents for Canadian boondocking (https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/how-to-find-free-camping-usa-canada/), which is the exact gravity muddytires is built to capture.

### Honest note — where rivals beat muddytires (so we can close it, not spin it)

- **Review depth & social proof.** The Dyrt (~4M reviews), Campendium (150k+ RVer reviews), and FreeCampsites (years of US reviews with cell-coverage/road-condition/busy-noisy notes) carry firsthand site-quality corpora our ~51k mostly-point POIs cannot match. *Closing move:* lean into the frictionless free contribution flow to grow Canadian reviews where incumbents are thin or absent.
- **Offline maps + native apps.** Outly and AllStays ship downloadable offline maps with live GPS positioning out of cell service — a core boondocking need a web map doesn't meet; Outly/Campendium/RV Parky have mature native iOS/Android apps with strong store ratings. *Closing move:* an offline-capable PWA or native wrapper is the highest-value parity gap for the actual use case (being off-grid).
- **RV-specific depth.** Campendium, AllStays, and RV Parky carry hookup/rig-length/dump/propane data and RV-safe routing by height/weight muddytires doesn't focus on. *Closing move:* fine to cede pure-RV-park depth; defend the vanlife/dispersed/Crown-land niche instead of chasing big-rig parity.
- **Booking & discounts.** The Dyrt's commission-free booking, discounts, and availability alerts are a transactional layer muddytires lacks. *Closing move:* not core to the free-Crown-land mission; deprioritize unless it becomes a revenue path.

*All claims above retain their source URL; `(inference)` marks anything reasoned rather than directly sourced.*
